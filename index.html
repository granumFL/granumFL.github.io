What it does

Debris flows as grains and water, not one fluid

Depth-averaged models replace boulders and water by one continuum. GranumFL keeps the grains as discrete elements and the water as SPH particles, so the boulders that dam a channel, the pore water that drains from a deposit and the erosion of a bed all come out of the physics.

⇄

Unresolved DEM–SPH coupling

The locally averaged formulation of Robinson, Ramaioli and Luding (2014): grains smaller than the smoothing length, a porosity field, Di Felice drag and −V∇P on each grain, with the exchanged momentum balanced to round-off.

▦

Both phases on one GPU

CUDA kernels compiled at run time with NVRTC (sm_120 included). The water reads the grains from the DEM buffers on the device, so a coupled step moves no particle data to the host. The same algorithm runs on the CPU in double precision.

⛰

Real terrain in a few clicks

Open a GeoTIFF or ESRI grid, or download the Copernicus GLO-30 tile of a latitude and longitude, crop, resample and cut. WGS 84 and UTM read from the file.

◎

Hazard maps and place records

Debris thickness, erosion and deposit, peak speed, flood depth by volume, runout and travel angle, and records at named places (thickness, speed, water depth, ρv²), as PNG maps or ESRI grids for GIS.

✦

An agent can drive it

A Model Context Protocol server built into the program. The in-app agent or any MCP client sets up the site, checks the warnings, runs or exports the case and reads the results.

☁

Large cases on a GPU server

Export a case for a CUDA server, run it with one command and load its results folder back into the app. A million grains over 150 s of flow ran in 63 min on one RTX 5090.

Architecture

The app and the agent call the same functions

An engineer works in the desktop app or asks the agent; both reach the site layer, which lays out the case for the coupled solver. Large cases go to a GPU server and come back as a results folder.

Real terrain

From a GeoTIFF to a site study

  1. Terrain. Open an elevation model or download the GLO-30 tile, crop by bounds or by a point and a size, and cut the terrain at the cell size you choose.
  2. Source & path. Draw the source and the catchment on the plan map, trace the steepest-descent path, lay an erodible bed and channel water along it, and name places.
  3. Soil and water. Start from a material preset and a Manning surface, and read the estimates and warnings, such as a water layer thinner than two particle spacings.
  4. Run and read. Run small cases locally or export to a GPU server, then read thickness, peak speed and flood maps, place records and the travel angle.

Copernicus GLO-30 is a surface model: canopy and buildings are in its heights, so a lidar terrain model gives better flow paths where one exists.

Agentic

Twenty tools, one request

In four independent trials an LLM agent, given only the engineer's request and the MCP tools, built or edited the 2026 Patong (Phuket) debris-flow site and passed all 33 objective checks, scored from the models it left. Where it falls short

      
    
        
  • A single settling grain is accurate only for 2.5 ≤ h/d ≤ 4: its own volume lowers the porosity it sees at small h/d, and a truncated kernel weakens its buoyancy at large h/d (+8.9 % at h/d = 6, on two GPUs).
    • 
        
  • A dense fixed bed gives 1.5 to 3.3 times Ergun's pressure drop at h/d = 2, several times the code's own Di Felice drag, so quick conditions and liquefied beds are not yet quantitative.
    • 
        
  • Field cases need the coarse-grained friction, rolling resistance and cohesion calibrated against an observed runout.
    • 
      

    

    

      
      
      
    

  






  

    

      
GPU performance

      
Production-class speed on one card

      
The water phase sustains 1.6 to 1.9×10⁸ particle updates per second from 0.2 to 12.2 million particles on an RTX 5090, 55 to 66 times 30 CPU threads of an EPYC 7502. The coupled phase advances 3.5 to 4.8×10⁷ grain steps per second up to 4.6 million grains.

      

      
For scale only: the DualSPHysics run of Crespo et al. (2015) on a GTX Titan corresponds to at least 2.9×10⁷ particle updates per second; that was older hardware and a mature, optimised code.

Paper

      
GranumFL: an agentic, GPU-accelerated coupled DEM–SPH program for debris flows on real terrain

      
Sompote Youwai, King Mongkut's University of Technology Thonburi, 2026. The paper describes the unresolved DEM–SPH coupling on one CUDA GPU, the calibration of each phase against published benchmarks (Chung and Ooi impacts within 0.09 % of Hertz, DEM8 penetration within the spread of eight open-source codes, a sedimenting porous block closer to theory than the reference implementation), the cases where the coupling is not yet accurate, the GPU performance, and the agent trials that set up a site study through 20 MCP tools.
